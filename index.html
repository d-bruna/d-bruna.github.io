02 · Machine learning, Erasmus, 2025

Day one is not year one

With three co-authors I classified 881 US IPOs by whether they beat the S&P 500, at a one-day and a one-year horizon, using four models and 18 pre-IPO features.

In our sample three in four IPOs beat the index on day one; after a year fewer than half did. The features that matter flip too: offer price drops from rank 1 to rank 8, profit margin climbs from 6 to 2, and institutional ownership is the only feature that stays important at both horizons.

Forecasting the risk itself

The hybrids were benchmarked against standalone GARCH-family and HAR models across assets and market regimes, using rolling-window significance tests rather than a single lucky split.

04 · Research, Erasmus School of Economics, 2026

When orthogonality fails to protect

Double Machine Learning promises that first-stage ML error won't contaminate a causal estimate. We tested that promise with a 648-cell Monte Carlo study, about 63 hours of compute, plus FRED-MD and Barro-Lee experiments.

Under dense, weak signals, Lasso-based DML coverage collapses to 35% while Ridge holds 69%, and more data widens the gap, from 9 points at n = 300 to 34 at n = 1,000. Comparing out-of-fold R² from both learners flags the risk before you trust an estimate.

06 · ML benchmarking platform, 2026

Infrastructure that keeps you honest

One shared interface for classical and quantum models: train, track in MLflow, distribute with Ray, serve with FastAPI, packaged in Docker. Any model swaps in without touching the pipeline.

The benchmark layer runs cross-validation, significance tests and noise sweeps. Its verdict on the test problem: classical models still win, and the reasons are well understood. Caching quantum-kernel evaluations mattered more than parallelising them.

07 · Now · TU Delft and Fugro

Accept the booking, or hold capacity?

As a research assistant at TU Delft I'm working on network revenue management for an asset-light freight platform. The platform owns no trucks, trains or barges; it buys capacity from carriers and decides which shipper requests to accept across modes.

A profitable request now can be the wrong call if it uses capacity that a better request needs later. That opportunity cost of capacity is what we study. Alongside it I build forecasting and MLflow workflows at Fugro.
